Enterprise AI product leadership

I lead AI products — specifically conversational and agentic systems — in enterprise settings. That work spans the full product lifecycle: identifying which problems are genuinely worth solving with AI, designing systems that behave reliably enough to trust in production, navigating the organizational and technical challenges of delivery, and building toward adoption rather than just launch.

The work is technical — I engage deeply with architecture, evaluation frameworks, and implementation tradeoffs — but it's grounded in product thinking. What does the system need to do, for whom, and how will we know if it's working? Those questions come first. Capability is in service of them.

I've worked across enterprise contexts where the stakes for AI reliability are high: inconsistent output is a user experience failure, but it can also be an operational failure with real downstream consequences. That shapes how I approach design and evaluation — with a bias toward measurable behavior and honest assessment of maturity.

Hands-on building

I build AI systems directly — not just specify them. That's deliberate. The gap between a reasonable-sounding product decision and what's actually achievable with current models, latency, and cost constraints is wide, and it's not something you can fully understand without touching the code.

Construction OS is the most complete example of this: a system I designed and am building myself, covering product, system architecture, and implementation. Working on it has shaped how I think about conversational AI design, context management, and the tradeoffs between capability and reliability in production.

I also build smaller experiments and prototypes — evaluation frameworks, intent routing systems, document processing pipelines — as a way of testing ideas and staying current with how the landscape is changing. These are described honestly in Demos: what they are, what I built, their maturity, and their limitations.

Construction industry background

My interest in AI for construction comes from direct industry experience — not just pattern-matching from adjacent domains. I understand the operational realities of construction: how information actually flows between field and office, what project data looks like in practice, where the genuine friction is and why existing software often doesn't solve it.

That firsthand context is what Construction OS is built on, and it's what distinguishes construction AI work I engage in from generic AI consulting applied to a new vertical. The problem formulation matters. AI applied to a poorly understood problem doesn't produce good outcomes, however good the technology is.

How I work

I work directly with clients on real problems — not through layers of account management, and not in a way that inflates the engagement beyond what the problem actually requires. The goal is to leave you with a better system and a clearer understanding of what to do next, not a dependency on me.

I'm candid about the limits of current AI technology, about what's genuinely achievable in a given context, and about where I see risk or unrealistic expectations in a proposed approach. That candor is more valuable to clients than enthusiasm — and it's how I'd want to be treated if I were the client.

If you want to understand more about how I approach a specific type of engagement, the Work with me page describes the two primary tracks and what working together typically involves.
